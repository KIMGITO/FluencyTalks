# Fluency Talks — Application Features and Business Value

## Purpose of This Document

Features of the Fluency Talks application

## 1. Account Registration and Sign-In

**What it does**  
Allows people to create an account and securely sign in using an email address or a supported social account. Users can also recover access if they forget their password.

**How it helps**  
Gives each learner a personal account where their profile, language choices, conversations, saved phrases, learning history, and points can be kept separate from those of other users. This makes it possible for users to return to their learning activities without starting over each time.

## 2. First-Time Setup and Language Profile

**What it does**  
Guides new users through a short setup process. They choose the language they speak natively, the language they want to learn, and their current level in that learning language, from A1 to C2. Users also confirm the required age, privacy, and terms-of-use declarations.

**How it helps**  
Gives the application the information it needs to recommend relevant language partners and tailor the learning experience. Language level helps other users understand a learner's current ability and decide how best to communicate with them.

## 3. Supported Language List

**What it does**  
Provides a searchable list of supported languages. 40 major world languages are offered at launch, including English, Vietnamese and Swahili. Language names can be displayed in their native form, and the list identifies languages currently offered for learning.

**How it helps**  
Makes it easier for users to find and select the right language, including languages they may know by a local or native name. A shared list also helps language choices remain consistent across profiles, partner discovery, conversations, corrections, and saved learning materials.

## 4. Home Feed and Language-Partner Suggestions

**What it does**  
Shows users a ranked list of potential language partners based on their language profiles. A perfect swap ranks first, then native speakers of the language the user is learning (whatever they are learning, even nothing), then anyone learning the user's native language. A swap is never required.

**How it helps**  
Reduces the effort required to find a suitable conversation partner. Instead of searching randomly, learners can start with people who can teach them, or people they can help. Both at once ranks highest, but one is enough.

**Current limitation**  
The feed merges teachers and learners into one list, labelled by reason.

## 5. Search for People and Messages

**What it does**  
Lets users search for other people by name or username and narrow results using available filters, including language and learning level. Users can also search the messages in their own conversations to find something that was discussed previously.

**How it helps**  
Helps learners locate a particular partner instead of relying only on suggested matches. Message search makes previous explanations, corrections, and useful expressions easier to find again without scrolling through an entire conversation.

## 6. Private Conversations and Messaging

**What it does**  
Allows two users to start a conversation, exchange text messages, reply to one another, and receive new messages while a conversation is open. Users may receive a conversation request and choose whether to accept or reject it. They can also see read information, react to messages, and edit or delete their own messages.

**How it helps**  
Creates the main space where language exchange happens. Learners can practise naturally, ask questions, explain expressions, and build an ongoing relationship with a partner. Conversation requests give users some control over who can begin a chat, while read information helps them understand whether a message has been seen.

## 7. Inline Language Corrections

**What it does**  
Allows an eligible language partner to suggest a correction to a sentence inside a conversation. The learner can compare the original wording with the suggested version, read an explanatory note, and accept or dismiss the correction. The comparison highlights what changed.

**How it helps**  
Turns everyday conversation into a learning opportunity. Learners can see the difference between what they wrote and a suggested improvement, understand the reason for the change, and keep a record of accepted corrections. Users who help with accepted corrections can earn points.

## 8. Personal Phrasebook

**What it does**  
Lets users save useful words, phrases, or expressions from conversations for later review. Saved items can include a translation and language information. Users can browse their saved phrases and remove items they no longer need.

**How it helps**  
Prevents useful expressions from being lost in long conversations. Learners build a personal collection of vocabulary and examples drawn from real interactions, making it easier to revisit and practise what they have learned.

## 9. Translation and Translation History

**What it does**  
Allows users to request a translation of a word, phrase, or message into another supported language. Translations can be saved in a personal history, reviewed later, and deleted individually.

**How it helps**  
Helps users understand messages when they encounter unfamiliar vocabulary or sentence structures. Keeping a history allows them to return to previous translations instead of repeatedly looking up the same material.

**Important note**  
Translation availability and usage may be subject to the limits of the translation service. The app's privacy information should explain that text submitted for translation is sent to an external translation provider.

## 10. Points and Rewards

**What it does**  
Gives users a points balance and a history showing points earned or spent. Users can earn points when their language corrections are accepted and can spend points on certain community-help requests.

**How it helps**  
Recognizes the time and effort users put into helping other learners. A visible balance and transaction history make rewards easier to understand and provide a record of why points were added or deducted.

## 11. Bounties — Requests for Extra Help

**What it does**  
Lets a learner attach a points reward to a question or message that needs an answer. Eligible speakers can view open requests and submit an answer. The learner can accept the answer, which awards the points to the helper. If no answer is accepted within 48 hours, or the question is deleted, the points are refunded. A user can have up to three open bounties at a time.

**How it helps**  
Gives learners a way to draw attention to a question they particularly need help with. The reward encourages knowledgeable community members to spend time answering, while the acceptance step lets the learner decide whether the answer resolves the question.

## 12. User Reporting and Blocking

**What it does**  
Allows users to report another user or concerning content for reasons such as harassment, spam, scams, impersonation, inappropriate behaviour, or suspicious corrections. Users can also block someone they do not want to interact with. Reports retain relevant information for review.

**How it helps**  
Gives users practical ways to respond to unwanted or unsafe interactions. Blocking helps prevent unwanted contact and hides the blocked person from relevant discovery areas. Reporting gives moderators information they can review and act on.

## 13. Moderation and Account Enforcement

**What it does**  
Provides authorised moderators and administrators with tools to review reports, search for users, view recent moderation actions, dismiss or action reports, and set accounts to active, suspended, or banned status. The application records moderation actions. The current rules also automatically suspend an account that reaches five reports within seven days.

**How it helps**  
Helps the team respond to abuse consistently and maintain a more trustworthy learning community. A record of actions supports accountability, while suspension and banning provide ways to restrict accounts that breach the community's rules.

**Owner consideration**  
The automatic suspension rule should be reviewed as part of the moderation policy so that reports can be assessed fairly and the process for reviewing or reversing a suspension is clear.

## 14. Saved Language Partners

**What it does**  
Lets users bookmark people they would like to study with again. They can view their saved partners later and remove a person from the list when needed.

**How it helps**  
Makes it easier to return to useful learning relationships. Users do not need to search for the same person every time they want to practise again.

## 15. Community Phrase Library

**What it is intended to do**  
Allows eligible users to contribute useful phrases, translations, categories, context, and an accompanying image to a shared collection that can benefit other learners. Contributors can remove posts they created.

**How it helps**  
Can grow a shared learning resource based on useful expressions contributed by the community, allowing learners to benefit from knowledge beyond their own conversations.

**Current limitation**  
The contribution functionality exists, but a complete public screen for browsing and searching community contributions is still to be built. The shared library should therefore be treated as an incomplete feature until learners can discover and use those contributions through the application.

## 16. Notifications

**What it does**  
Alerts users to important activity, including conversation requests, corrections, accepted corrections, and paid bounties. A notification area shows recent notifications and indicates unread items. Users can mark notifications as read or delete them.

**How it helps**  
Keeps users informed when someone responds or when an action requires their attention. This can encourage learners to return to conversations, review corrections, and follow up on requests instead of missing important activity.

## 17. Profile and Personal Settings

**What it does**  
Lets users manage their display name, username, biography, country, time zone, privacy setting, and profile picture. Their profile helps other members understand who they are and what languages they are interested in.

**How it helps**  
Makes language-partner discovery more personal and informative. A clear profile can help users decide whom to contact, while privacy settings give them some control over how their profile is presented.

## 18. Account, Data, and Consent Management

**What it does**  
Allows users to request an export of their personal application data and permanently delete their account. The application also records agreement to its terms, privacy policy, and age requirement, including the version of the terms accepted.

**How it helps**  
Gives users greater control over their information and supports transparent handling of account data. Recording consent helps the project track which terms a user agreed to and identify when updated terms need to be presented.

## 19. Public Landing Page and Legal Information

**What it does**  
Provides a public page explaining what Fluency Talks is and what users can do with it, with a route to registration. Public legal pages provide the terms of use, privacy information, and information about data deletion.

**How it helps**  
Introduces the product to prospective users before they register and sets clear expectations about how the application works. Accessible legal information helps users understand the rules and how their data is handled.
