# Product overview

**Live site: [thefluencytalks.com](https://thefluencytalks.com)**

## What it is

The Fluency Talks is a **study-partner platform, not a social network**. A person declares the one language they speak natively and, optionally, the one they are learning. The app shows them native speakers of the language they are learning and learners of the language they speak.

A perfect swap ranks first but is never required. A Kenyan learning Vietnamese sees every Vietnamese speaker, even one who is learning nothing, and every learner of Swahili.

Partners chat, qualified speakers correct each other's sentences inline, learners save phrases to a personal phrasebook, and points reward helpful corrections. There are no follower counts, no likes and no popularity contest.

## Who it's for

- **Language learners** at any level from A1 to C2: students abroad, expats, anyone studying a language.
- **Native and fluent speakers** who want to help, and earn recognition for it.
- Everyone must be 16 or older.

## The story in one example

Tom speaks English natively and is learning Vietnamese at A2. Linh, in Hanoi, speaks Vietnamese natively and is learning English. They match.

1. Tom writes *Tôi là đói*.
2. Linh corrects it to *Tôi đói*, and Tom sees the change in red and green. In Vietnamese, words like "hungry" work like verbs and take no "to be".
3. Tom accepts. Linh earns 5 points, and the corrected phrase lands in Tom's phrasebook.
4. Later, Tom is stuck on an exam phrase. He spends 50 points on a bounty, and a native speaker answers it.

## Principles

1. **Conversation first.** Text chat now. Voice and video could come later, behind the same people and block model, but they aren't promised.
2. **Safe by design.** Blocking, reporting and message requests shipped before chat went live.
3. **Earned, not claimed.** Levels rise from real activity, and only verified levels unlock the right to correct others.
4. **One real design system.** Every colour, font, size, spacing value and gradient lives in one file.
5. **Component-driven.** Pages only compose components. If you write the same markup twice, make a component.
6. **Responsive.** Three columns on desktop, two on tablet, and one with a bottom tab bar on phones.
7. **Portable.** Supabase today, and plain Postgres plus our own backend could follow. See [Architecture](ARCHITECTURE.md#portability).
8. **Honest claims.** Every public statement describes something the app really does.

## What it isn't

No voice or video, paid tutors, courses, certificates, native iOS or Android apps, AI tutor, or "fluent fast" promises. It isn't a dating or friend-making service either.
